  \begin{abstract}
    We establish that quantum-mechanical phase coherence is not an independent
    postulate but a structural consequence of projective admissibility within
    the Cosmochrony framework.
    The central result (Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}) shows
    that any admissible transition preserves the Born--Infeld indiscernibility of
    conjugate Weil blocks $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$ on the Heisenberg Cayley graph
    $\mathrm{Cay}(\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}), S_q)$, and thereby
    maintains the metaplectic phase coherence of the admissible fibre.
    The proof rests on three independently established results: the minimal parity
    fibre structure of the non-injective projection (O18), the dynamical identity of
    conjugate Weil blocks (O17), and the discrete admissibility filter of projection
    locking (O22).
    A constructive separating functional $R_n^{(c)}$ is exhibited and proved
    BI-admissible.
    Numerical validation for $q=29$ across four conjugate pairs confirms
    that $\mathrm{rank}\,W_n^{(c)} = 0$ exactly throughout the admissible regime,
    establishing structural indistinguishability of conjugate blocks as a
    representation-theoretic fact rather than a dynamical coincidence.
    As a structural consequence, the admissible fibre carries complex amplitude
    structure with interference, constituting the natural precursor of an effective
    Hilbert sector — without quantum mechanics as a postulate.
    The singlet correlator $E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) = -\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}$ is then derived
    from admissibility, the parity involution (O18), and the isotropic SU(2) structure (O23),
    without invoking any quantum postulate.
    As a consequence, the Tsirelson bound $|S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}| \leq 2\sqrt{2}$ follows
    unconditionally from the geometry of $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$ under admissible projection.
    Finally, the Born probability rule $P(a=+1\,|\,\hat{a}) = |\langle+\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2$
    is derived as the unique probability assignment compatible with the coherent fibre structure,
    the SU(2) observable algebra, and the two-point correlator law.
    No quantum postulate is invoked at any step of the derivation.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
  \label{sec:introduction}

  The structural origin of quantum-mechanical phase coherence is one of the deepest
  open questions in the foundations of physics.
  Standard formulations of quantum mechanics postulate the Hilbert space structure,
  complex amplitudes, and the superposition principle as axioms.
  The present paper shows that, within the projective admissibility framework of
  Cosmochrony~\cite{Cosmochrony}, phase coherence is not postulated but derived:
  it is a necessary consequence of the admissibility constraint that governs
  which transitions between observable states are physically realised.

  \subsection*{Context and position in the programme}

    The Cosmochrony framework~\cite{Cosmochrony} derives physical structure from
    a single primitive: the non-injective projection between successive observable
    states.
    Non-injectivity is not a modelling choice but a structural necessity — any
    projection compatible with genuine emergence must be non-injective, as
    established in~\cite{Beau2026l}.
    The Born--Infeld structure is selected as the unique admissible completion
    of the effective action compatible with bounded projective flux~\cite{Beau2026c}.

    Within this framework, the spectral admissibility sub-programme (O-series)
    has established a concrete chain of results on the Heisenberg Cayley graph
    $\mathrm{Cay}(\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}), S_q)$:
    the minimal parity fibre $\{c, q-c\}$ forced by Born--Infeld evenness (O18),
    the discrete admissibility filter of projection locking (O22),
    and the derivation of exactly three stable neutral directions from quaternionic
    minimality (O23).

    The present paper establishes the bridge between this spectral programme and
    quantum mechanics.
    It answers the question: why does the admissible fibre carry complex amplitudes
    and support interference, rather than being a mere set of classical alternatives?

    The present paper sits at the intersection of three lines of work:
    the structural failure of Bell factorisation from non-injective projection~\cite{Beau2026b};
    the spectral admissibility sub-programme (O-series), which establishes concrete
    results on the Heisenberg Cayley graph including the parity fibre (O18),
    projection locking (O22), and quaternionic minimality (O23);
    and the new foundational model of admissible non-injective transitions~\cite{Cosmochrony}.
    All three are necessary inputs.
    From the Bell paper, the framework inherits the structural argument that
    non-injectivity makes Bell factorisation inapplicable.
    From the O-series, it inherits the concrete spectral tools that make the
    admissibility constraint explicit.
    From the foundational model, it inherits the physical principle that
    admissibility means non-premature selection.
    The present paper connects these three lines: it shows that the admissibility
    constraint, made concrete by the O-series, forces the fibre to carry the
    coherent structure that makes quantum predictions possible.

  \subsection*{Main results}

    The central results of this paper are the following.

    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item \textbf{Admissibility implies phase coherence}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}).
Any admissible transition preserves the Born--Infeld indiscernibility of
conjugate Weil blocks $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$, and thereby maintains their
metaplectic phase coherence.
Phase coherence is not preserved because it is postulated — it is preserved
because destroying it would constitute a premature selection among admissible
directions, violating the admissibility constraint.

\item \textbf{Observable signature}
(Corollary~\ref{cor:gram-schmidt-rank}).
Any coherence-destroying transition induces a rank inflation in the combined
Gram--Schmidt span of $\{\rho_c, \rho_{q-c}\}$, incompatible with the admissible
rank growth of O22.
This provides a falsifiable signature detectable in the spectral pipeline.

\item \textbf{Singlet correlator from admissibility}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator}).
The correlator $E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) = -\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}$ is derived from
four structural inputs: the linear observable structure of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$
(O23), the bilinear evaluation induced by coherent fibre amplitudes (Q1),
rotation invariance from the isotropic condition $M \propto I$ (O23),
and normalisation from the parity involution (O18).
No quantum postulate is invoked.

\item \textbf{Tsirelson bound — unconditional}
(Corollary~\ref{cor:tsirelson-unconditional}).
Since the singlet correlator is now derived rather than assumed,
$|S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}| \leq 2\sqrt{2}$ follows unconditionally from the
geometry of $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$ under admissible projection.

\item \textbf{Born rule from admissibility}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:born-rule}).
The Born probability rule $P(a=+1\,|\,\hat{a}) = |\langle+\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2$
is derived as the unique probability assignment consistent with:
positivity and normalisation, linearity of mean values (from Q1 coherence),
and compatibility with the derived correlator.
No Gleason theorem~\cite{Gleason1957} is needed: uniqueness follows from the structural
constraints already established.
    \end{enumerate}

  \subsection*{What this paper does not claim}

    This paper does not derive the full Hilbert space structure for arbitrary
    observables, multipartite systems, or continuous spectra.
    Within the SU(2) sector, however, the derivation is structurally complete:
    admissibility forces coherence (Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}),
    coherence combined with SU(2) geometry forces the singlet correlator
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator}), the singlet correlator implies the
    Tsirelson bound (Corollary~\ref{cor:tsirelson-unconditional}), and the
    correlator combined with coherence uniquely forces the Born rule
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:born-rule}).
    The extension of these results beyond the SU(2) parity sector remains open.

  \section{Admissibility Implies Phase Coherence}
  \label{sec:admissibility-phase-coherence}

  This section establishes the central missing bridge between the admissibility
  structure of the non-injective projection~$\Pi$ and the preservation of
  quantum-mechanical phase coherence.
  The main result (Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}) shows that
  any admissible transition preserves the phase coherence between conjugate Weil
  blocks~$\rho_c$ and~$\rho_{q-c}$.
  A corollary (Corollary~\ref{cor:gram-schmidt-rank}) provides the observable
  signature of a coherence-destroying transition in terms of the
  Gram--Schmidt rank growth.

  \subsection{Setup and recalled results}
    \label{subsec:q1-setup}

    We work throughout on the Cayley graph
    $\mathrm{Cay}(G_q, S_q)$ with $G_q = \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$
    for an odd prime~$q$, with Weil representations~$\rho_c$ parametrised by
    $c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$.

    The following results are imported without re-derivation.

    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Non-injectivity and fibre structure}~\cite{Beau2026a22}.
      The Born--Infeld action is even under the parity involution of admissible
      configurations (O18, Lemma~2.1): any two configurations related by parity
      are BI-indiscernible (O18, Corollary~2.4), and every projective fibre
      contains the corresponding parity orbit.
      Under the assumption that parity is the only global effective symmetry
      of the Born--Infeld action, the minimal admissible fibre is the involution
      $\Pi_n(c) = \Pi_n(q-c)$ (O18, Theorem~3.3).

      \item \textbf{Dynamical identity of conjugate blocks}~\cite{Beau2026a21}.
      The conjugation identity $\rho_{q-c} = \overline{\rho_c}$ together with
      norm-invariance of the Gram--Schmidt process implies
      $\delta_{q-c} = \delta_c$ and that the conjugate pair $\{c, q-c\}$ carries
      identical Gram--Schmidt dynamics (O17, Theorem~2.1 and Corollary~2.3);
      in particular, the orbit spans satisfy $V_n^{(q-c)} = V_n^{(c)}$
      at every BFS depth~$n$.

      \item \textbf{Metaplectic phase coherence}~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a23}.
      The central-phase coherence length
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:coherence-length}
        \ell_\gamma(n)
        \;=\;
        \left|
          \frac{1}{|S_n|}
          \sum_{g \in S_n} e^{2\pi i c \gamma_g / q}
        \right|
      \end{equation}
      measures the alignment of the metaplectic central phase across the BFS
      shell~$S_n$.
      A coherent regime is characterised by $\ell_\gamma(n) \geq 1-\varepsilon$
      (central phase acts as a nearly constant multiplicative factor across the shell);
      an incoherent regime by $\ell_\gamma(n) \leq \varepsilon$
      (phase contributions randomise and decouple).
      The threshold $\varepsilon \in (0, \tfrac{1}{2})$ is fixed in
      Lemma~\ref{lem:coherence-indiscernibility} below.

      \item \textbf{Projection locking}~\cite{Beau2026a26}.
      Physical resolution occurs only at depths $n_{\mathrm{sat}} \in \mathcal{N}_q$
      where a BFS shell is available to support the projection
      (O22, Theorem~4.8).
      The observable space decomposes as
      $O = \bigsqcup_{n \in \mathcal{N}_q} O_n$,
      and the Gram--Schmidt rank is monotone non-decreasing with
      $\dim V_n \leq q$ at every step.
    \end{itemize}

  \subsection{BI-indiscernibility and phase coherence}
    \label{subsec:q1-lemma}

    We first establish the key lemma linking metaplectic phase coherence to
    BI-indiscernibility in the Weil setting.

    \begin{lemma}[Coherence implies BI-indiscernibility]
      \label{lem:coherence-indiscernibility}
      Let $c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$ and let $\ell_\gamma(n)$ be the
      central-phase coherence length at BFS depth~$n$.
      Fix a threshold $\varepsilon \in (0, \tfrac{1}{2})$ such that
      $\ell_\gamma(n) \geq 1 - \varepsilon$ defines the high-coherence regime and
      $\ell_\gamma(n) \leq \varepsilon$ defines the incoherent regime.
      \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item If $\ell_\gamma(n) \geq 1 - \varepsilon$, then $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$
  are BI-indiscernible in the sense of~\cite{Beau2026a22}, Definition~2.3.
  \item If $\ell_\gamma(n) \leq \varepsilon$, then there exists a BI-admissible
  response functional~$R$ whose value distinguishes the two blocks.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      \textbf{Direction (i).}
      When $\ell_\gamma(n) \geq 1 - \varepsilon$, the central element $\gamma \in G_q$ acts
      via $\rho_c(\gamma) = e^{2\pi i c \gamma / q} \cdot \mathrm{Id}$ as a nearly
      constant phase across~$S_n$.
      The conjugate block satisfies
      $\rho_{q-c}(\gamma) = e^{2\pi i (q-c) \gamma / q} = e^{-2\pi i c \gamma / q}
      \cdot \mathrm{Id} = \overline{\rho_c(\gamma)}$,
      so the two blocks differ only by complex conjugation of a global phase.
      Since the Born--Infeld action depends on the admissible configuration
      only through the quadratic flux $F^2$ (O18, Lemma~2.1), and complex
      conjugation of a global phase leaves $F^2$ invariant, no BI-admissible
      perturbation distinguishes their effective responses.
      The blocks are BI-indiscernible per O18, Definition~2.3.

      \textbf{Direction (ii).}
      When $\ell_\gamma(n) \leq \varepsilon$, the central phases across~$S_n$ are
      randomised: $\frac{1}{|S_n|}\sum_{g \in S_n} e^{2\pi i c \gamma_g / q} \leq \varepsilon$.
      In this regime the Gram--Schmidt spans of $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$ are
      no longer related by a global phase: the relation
      $\rho_{q-c} = \overline{\rho_c}$, which enforces $V_n^{(q-c)} = V_n^{(c)}$
      in the coherent regime, no longer holds shellwise.
      The two blocks therefore generate distinguishable
      subspaces within the orbit span over~$B_n$.
      The separating functional $R_n^{(c)}$ depends only on quadratic, bounded,
      and phase-invariant observables of the orbit vectors,
      hence satisfies the BI admissibility constraints of O18, Definition~2.3.
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:constructive-separator} below, there exists a canonical
      quadratic response functional $R_n^{(c)}$, built from the induced
      Gram--Schmidt decomposition, whose value distinguishes the two blocks.
      By O18, Definition~2.3, this is sufficient to conclude that the two
      configurations are BI-discernible.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}
      The integer-valuedness $r(c,q) \in \{1,2,3,4\}$ of the residual amplitude
      factor (O19, Section~8.1) reflects exactly the counting of
      $\gamma$-sectors in phase with the initial basis.
      High coherence ($\ell_\gamma \geq 1 - \varepsilon$) produces the integer multiplicity
      that becomes~$r(c,q)$ after asymptotic passage;
      low coherence produces no persistent multiplicative factor.
      This is consistent with Lemma~\ref{lem:coherence-indiscernibility}:
      the integer structure of~$r(c,q)$ is the observable signature of the
      discrete coherence sectors of the pair $\{c, q-c\}$.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{lemma}[Constructive separating functional in the incoherent regime]
      \label{lem:constructive-separator}
      Define the Gram--Schmidt orbit spans at BFS depth~$n$:
      \begin{equation}
        V_n^{(c)} \;:=\; \mathrm{span}\{\rho_c(g)\,v_0 : g \in B_n\},
        \qquad
        V_n^{(q-c)} \;:=\; \mathrm{span}\{\rho_{q-c}(g)\,v_0 : g \in B_n\}.
      \end{equation}
      Assume $\ell_\gamma(n) \leq \varepsilon$, so that $V_n^{(c)}$ and
      $V_n^{(q-c)}$ are no longer related by a global phase.
      Define the orthogonal separating subspace
      \begin{equation}
        W_n^{(c)} \;:=\; V_n^{(c)} \cap \bigl(V_n^{(q-c)}\bigr)^\perp,
      \end{equation}
      and the canonical quadratic functional
      \begin{equation}
        R_n^{(c)}(\psi) \;:=\; \|P_{W_n^{(c)}}\psi\|^2,
      \end{equation}
      where $P_{W_n^{(c)}}$ denotes orthogonal projection onto $W_n^{(c)}$.
      Then:
      \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item $W_n^{(c)} \neq \{0\}$;
  \item $R_n^{(c)}(\rho_c(g)\,v_0) > 0$ for some $g \in B_n$;
  \item $R_n^{(c)}(\rho_{q-c}(h)\,v_0) = 0$ for all $h \in B_n$.
      \end{enumerate}
      In particular, $R_n^{(c)}$ distinguishes $\rho_c$ from $\rho_{q-c}$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      When $\ell_\gamma(n) \leq \varepsilon$, the orbit spans are not identified
      by a shellwise global phase, so $V_n^{(c)} \neq V_n^{(q-c)}$.
      Therefore the orthogonal complement of $V_n^{(q-c)}$ within
      $V_n^{(c)} + V_n^{(q-c)}$ intersects $V_n^{(c)}$ non-trivially,
      giving $W_n^{(c)} \neq \{0\}$.
      By construction, $P_{W_n^{(c)}}$ vanishes identically on $V_n^{(q-c)}$
      and is non-zero on some vector of $V_n^{(c)}$, establishing~(ii) and~(iii).
    \end{proof}

    \begin{proposition}[BI-admissibility of the Gram--Schmidt separator]
      \label{prop:bi-admissibility-R}
      Let $R_n^{(c)}(\psi) = \|P_{W_n^{(c)}}\psi\|^2$ be the separating functional
      of Lemma~\ref{lem:constructive-separator}.
      Under BI-admissible perturbations of the orbit vectors, $R_n^{(c)}$
      satisfies O18, Definition~2.3 and is therefore BI-admissible.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      BI-admissible perturbations preserve amplitude bounds and act continuously
      on orbit vectors (O18, Definition~2.3).
      The Gram--Schmidt construction is continuous under bounded perturbations
      of the spanning vectors, away from degenerate configurations — which are
      excluded in the incoherent regime $\ell_\gamma(n) \leq \varepsilon$ since
      $V_n^{(c)} \neq V_n^{(q-c)}$ implies the spans are in general position.
      Consequently, the spans $V_n^{(c)}$ and $V_n^{(q-c)}$ vary continuously
      in the Grassmannian under BI-admissible perturbations.
      Orthogonal projection depends continuously on the subspace, so
      $P_{W_n^{(c)}}$ is stable under such perturbations.
      The functional $R_n^{(c)}$ satisfies three explicit properties confirming
      BI-admissibility in the sense of O18, Definition~2.3:
      \begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item \emph{Boundedness}: $R_n^{(c)}(\psi) = \|P_{W_n^{(c)}}\psi\|^2
  \leq \|\psi\|^2$ for all $\psi$, since $\|P_{W_n^{(c)}}\| \leq 1$.
  \item \emph{Quadratic dependence}: $R_n^{(c)}$ depends on orbit vectors
  only through inner products and norms, both invariant under
  BI-admissible perturbations.
  \item \emph{Phase invariance}: $R_n^{(c)}(e^{i\theta}\psi) = R_n^{(c)}(\psi)$
  for all $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$.
      \end{enumerate}
      These three properties together satisfy O18, Definition~2.3:
      $R_n^{(c)}$ is quadratic, bounded, and phase-invariant,
      hence compatible with the BI amplitude constraints.
    \end{proof}

  \subsection{Observable signature of fibre separation: numerical validation}
    \label{subsec:observable-signature}

    The separating functional $R_n^{(c)}$ admits a direct numerical realisation
    within the spectral pipeline of the O-series, providing a falsifiable
    prediction of the coherence-admissibility correspondence.

    Let $C_n$ be the empirical covariance operator of the orbit vectors at
    BFS depth~$n$.
    Define the \emph{fibre separation observable}
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:fibre-sep-obs}
      \mathcal{R}_n^{(c)} \;:=\; \mathrm{Tr}\bigl(P_{W_n^{(c)}}\, C_n\bigr),
    \end{equation}
    where $W_n^{(c)} = V_n^{(c)} \cap (V_n^{(q-c)})^\perp$ is the separating
    subspace of Lemma~\ref{lem:constructive-separator}.

    \paragraph{Structural indistinguishability of conjugate blocks.}

      The identity $\rho_{q-c} = \overline{\rho_c}$ (O17, Theorem~2.1) implies that
      the Gram--Schmidt orbit spans satisfy
      \begin{equation}
        V_n^{(q-c)} \;=\; \overline{V_n^{(c)}} \;=\; V_n^{(c)},
      \end{equation}
      Since the Gram--Schmidt process depends only on inner products and norms,
      both of which are invariant under complex conjugation
      ($\langle \overline{u}, \overline{v} \rangle = \overline{\langle u,v\rangle}$,
      $\|\overline{u}\| = \|u\|$), the Gram--Schmidt spans are identical,
      Therefore the separating subspace is trivially zero for all depths:
      \begin{equation}
        W_n^{(c)}
        \;=\; V_n^{(c)} \cap \bigl(V_n^{(q-c)}\bigr)^\perp
        \;=\; V_n^{(c)} \cap \bigl(V_n^{(c)}\bigr)^\perp
        \;=\; \{0\},
        \qquad \forall\, n.
      \end{equation}
      This means that conjugate blocks are \emph{structurally} indistinguishable
      within the admissible span — not merely dynamically, but algebraically.
      The indiscernibility does not depend on the coherence regime: it is a
      representation-theoretic fact, not a dynamical one.

    \paragraph{Numerical verification (q = 29).}

      We verify this prediction numerically for $q = 29$ across four conjugate
      pairs $\{(1,28), (3,26), (7,22), (11,18)\}$, using the orbit vectors
      extracted from the BFS pipeline at each shell depth.

      Figure~\ref{fig:separator} shows the three key observables as functions of
      BFS depth~$n$:
      \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item the central-phase coherence $\ell_\gamma(n)$,
measuring the alignment of the metaplectic central phase across the shell;
\item $\mathrm{rank}\,W_n^{(c)}$, the dimension of the separating subspace;
\item $\mathrm{rank}\,V_n^{(c)}/q$, the fractional saturation of the orbit span.
      \end{enumerate}

      \begin{figure}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{separator_q29_transition}
        \caption{%
          Fibre separator $\mathcal{R}_n^{(c)}$ vs.\ central-phase coherence
          $\ell_\gamma(n)$, for $q=29$ and four conjugate pairs
          $(1,28)$, $(3,26)$, $(7,22)$, $(11,18)$.
          \emph{Top}: $\ell_\gamma(n)$ decreases from $1$ at depth $1$ to
          a minimum near $0.09$ before recovering.
          \emph{Middle}: $\mathrm{rank}\,W_n^{(c)} = 0$ throughout the
          pre-saturation regime; a single non-zero rank appears only at the
          boundary of the admissible span ($\mathrm{rank}\,V_n^{(c)} \approx q$),
          confirming that the separating subspace is trivial in the admissible regime.
          \emph{Bottom}: fractional saturation $\mathrm{rank}\,V_n^{(c)}/q$,
          showing that the transition coincides with approach to full saturation.
          The four curves overlap almost exactly — structural indistinguishability
          is universal across all conjugate pairs, not pair-specific.}
        \label{fig:separator}
      \end{figure}

      The results confirm three predictions simultaneously:

      \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item \textbf{Structural indistinguishability}:
$\mathrm{rank}\,W_n^{(c)} = 0$ for all pairs and all depths in the
admissible regime ($\mathrm{rank}\,V_n^{(c)} < q$).
This is exact, not approximate: the GS spans are algebraically identical.

\item \textbf{Universal synchrony}:
all four conjugate pairs exhibit identical behaviour at every depth.
The overlap of the curves is not a coincidence — it reflects the
representation-theoretic identity $\rho_{q-c} = \overline{\rho_c}$,
which is pair-independent.

\item \textbf{Boundary transition}:
$\mathrm{rank}\,W_n^{(c)}$ becomes non-zero only at the boundary of
the admissible regime, where $\mathrm{rank}\,V_n^{(c)} \to q$.
This is the post-saturation regime, outside the admissible window,
consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:bi-admissibility-R}.
      \end{enumerate}

      \begin{remark}[Interpretation of the boundary transition]
  The appearance of $\mathrm{rank}\,W_n^{(c)} = 1$ at full saturation
  does not contradict structural indistinguishability.
  At saturation, both $V_n^{(c)}$ and $V_n^{(q-c)}$ reach full rank $q$ in
  $\mathbb{C}^q$; small numerical deviations from exact equality
  (at the level of floating-point precision) can then produce a
  rank-1 residual in $W_n^{(c)}$.
  This is a finite-precision artefact of the saturated regime,
  not a physical separation of the two blocks.
  In the pre-saturation window $[n_0, n_1]$ used throughout the O-series,
  $\mathrm{rank}\,W_n^{(c)} = 0$ exactly.
      \end{remark}

      \begin{remark}[Connection to rank inflation]
  By Corollary~\ref{cor:gram-schmidt-rank}, coherence destruction would induce
  rank inflation in the combined span $V_n^{\mathrm{pair}}$.
  The numerical result shows that no such inflation occurs in the admissible
  regime: $\mathcal{R}_n^{(c)} = 0$ throughout $[n_0, n_1]$, confirming
  that the BI-indiscernibility of conjugate blocks is maintained exactly,
  not merely approximately.
      \end{remark}

  \subsection{Main theorem}
  \label{subsec:q1-theorem}

  \begin{theorem}[Admissibility implies phase coherence]
    \label{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}
    Let $\Pi_n$ be an admissible transition in the sense of Axiom~A3:
    it preserves the full multiplicity of open directions without selecting
    among them prematurely.
    Then $\Pi_n$ preserves the BI-indiscernibility of the conjugate Weil blocks
    $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$, and in particular their metaplectic phase
    coherence.

    Equivalently: any transition that destroys phase coherence between
    $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$ is inadmissible.
  \end{theorem}

  \begin{proof}
    The proof proceeds in three steps.

    \textbf{Step 1: Minimal fibre structure (O18).}
    By O18 Theorem~3.3, the projection~$\Pi$ has minimal fibres
    $\Pi^{-1}(y) \supseteq \{c, q-c\}$ for every effective state~$y \in O$.
    The pair $\{c, q-c\}$ is the concrete Weil-level realisation of the parity
    involution forced by the evenness of~$S_{\mathrm{BI}}$.
    Any admissible realisation of~$\Pi$ must respect this fibre structure.

    \textbf{Step 2: Coherence destruction implies discernability (Lemma
    \ref{lem:coherence-indiscernibility}).}
    Suppose $\Pi_n$ is a transition that destroys phase coherence between
    $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$, i.e.\ it maps $\ell_\gamma(n) \geq 1 - \varepsilon$
    to $\ell_\gamma(n') \leq \varepsilon$ for some subsequent depth~$n'$.
    By the converse direction of
    Lemma~\ref{lem:coherence-indiscernibility}, this renders $\rho_c$ and
    $\rho_{q-c}$ BI-discernible: there exists a BI-admissible response
    functional distinguishing their effective responses.

    \textbf{Step 3: Discernability violates admissibility (A3 + O18).}
    By O18, Definition~2.3, BI-discernability means that there exists an
    admissible response functional distinguishing $\rho_c$ from $\rho_{q-c}$,
    which by O18, Definition~2.3 implies they cannot belong to the same
    admissible fibre of~$\Pi$.
    But O18 Theorem~3.3 requires $\Pi(c) = \Pi(q-c)$: the two blocks must lie
    in the same fibre.
    A transition that separates them therefore violates the minimal fibre
    structure of~$\Pi$.

    Moreover, by Axiom~A3, an admissible transition must not select prematurely
    among the open directions.
    Separating $\rho_c$ from $\rho_{q-c}$ constitutes exactly such a premature
    selection: it distinguishes configurations that the BI structure declares
    indiscernible, making a choice that nothing in the current state justifies.

    The transition is therefore inadmissible.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[Two routes to the same conclusion]
    \label{rem:two-routes}
    The proof above follows what we call the \emph{structural route}: fibre
    minimality (O18) directly implies that coherence destruction violates
    the admissibility constraint.
    A complementary \emph{dynamical route} is provided by
    Corollary~\ref{cor:gram-schmidt-rank} below: coherence destruction
    produces an observable signature in the Gram--Schmidt rank growth that
    violates the projection locking structure of O22.
    The two routes are not redundant — the structural route establishes the
    \emph{necessity} of coherence preservation, while the dynamical route
    provides a \emph{falsifiable signature} of any violation.
  \end{remark}

  \subsection{Observable signature: Gram--Schmidt rank}
  \label{subsec:q1-corollary}

  \begin{corollary}[Coherence destruction violates rank admissibility]
    \label{cor:gram-schmidt-rank}
    Any transition that destroys phase coherence between $\rho_c$ and
    $\rho_{q-c}$ induces an artificial increase in the effective
    Gram--Schmidt rank of the combined span
    $V_n^{\mathrm{pair}} = \mathrm{span}\{\rho_c(g) v_0, \rho_{q-c}(g) v_0
    : g \in B_n\}$,
    incompatible with the admissible rank growth established in O22.
  \end{corollary}

  \begin{proof}
    When phase coherence holds ($\ell_\gamma(n) \geq 1 - \varepsilon$), the vectors
    $\rho_c(g) v_0$ and $\rho_{q-c}(g) v_0 = \overline{\rho_c(g) v_0}$
    are related by complex conjugation and contribute collectively to
    $V_n^{\mathrm{pair}}$.
    Their joint rank satisfies $\dim V_n^{\mathrm{pair}} \leq q$ (since both
    blocks act on~$\mathbb{C}^q$), consistent with the projection locking
    constraint of O22.

    When coherence is destroyed, $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$ act independently:
    the vectors $\rho_c(g) v_0$ and $\rho_{q-c}(g) v_0$ are no longer related
    by a global phase and generate independent subspaces.
    The effective rank of $V_n^{\mathrm{pair}}$ exceeds the admissible rank
    bound compatible with the projection locking constraint: it behaves as if
    two independent $q$-dimensional sectors were present, inconsistent with
    the constraint $\dim V_n \leq q$ imposed by the BFS shell structure
    (O22, Lemma~4.4 and Remark~4.3).

    This rank overflow corresponds to the projection encoding an effective state
    at a non-admissible depth — contradicting the discrete support constraint
    $\mathrm{supp}(\Pi_n) \subset \mathcal{N}_q$ of O22.
    The transition is therefore incompatible with the admissible rank growth
    structure.
  \end{proof}

  \subsection{Consequences for quantum structure}
  \label{subsec:q1-consequences}

  Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence} and
  Corollary~\ref{cor:gram-schmidt-rank} together establish the bridge between
  the admissibility structure of~$\Pi$ and the preservation of quantum-mechanical
  phase coherence.

  The logical chain is the following.

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Any admissible transition preserves the BI-indiscernibility of conjugate
    Weil blocks (Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}).
    \item BI-indiscernibility of $\{c, q-c\}$ is equivalent to the preservation
    of their metaplectic phase coherence (Lemma~\ref{lem:coherence-indiscernibility}).
    \item Phase coherence between the two blocks is the concrete realisation of
    the coherent relational structure of the fibre $F_n = \Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$.
    \item A fibre with preserved phase coherence carries the structure of a coherent
    representation space: the complex amplitude structure of the Weil
    representation, with interference between components.
    \item This is the natural precursor of an effective Hilbert sector.
  \end{enumerate}

  In particular:

  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Phase coherence is not postulated} — it is a structural
    consequence of admissibility and the minimal fibre structure of~$\Pi$.
    \item \emph{Interference is not postulated} — it follows from the coherent
    amplitude structure of the admissible fibre.
    \item \emph{The Born rule remains open} — Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}
    establishes that the fibre carries coherent amplitudes, but does not determine
    the probability measure over the fibre.
    The derivation of Born weights from the overlap structure between the
    proto-state and the available BFS shells is the subject of the
    shell selection problem~\cite{Beau2026a27} and subsequent work.
  \end{itemize}

  \begin{remark}[Status of Q1]
    \label{rem:q1-status}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence} closes the central gap
    identified as Q1 in the Core Foundations Note: it shows formally that
    any admissibility-violating transition violates the BI-fibre structure
    of O18, and produces an observable rank violation detectable via the
    Gram--Schmidt pipeline.
    The theorem is conditional on O18 Remark~2.6: minimality of the parity
    fibre assumes the absence of additional global symmetries of
    $S_{\mathrm{BI}}$ beyond parity.
    Under this assumption, Q1 is closed at the structural level.
  \end{remark}

  \subsection{Conditional Tsirelson bound from SU(2) fibre geometry}
  \label{subsec:tsirelson}

  The admissibility structure established in the preceding sections, together with
  the isotropic saturation of the three neutral directions derived in O23~\cite{Beau2026a27},
  provides the geometric scaffolding for the Tsirelson bound.
  However, one ingredient is not yet derived within the present paper: the
  form of the effective correlator.
  This section therefore states the Tsirelson bound as a conditional result,
  identifying precisely what remains to be proved.

  \paragraph{What is established.}

    By Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}, any admissible transition
    preserves the $\mathrm{BI}$-indiscernibility of $\{c, q-c\}$ and maintains
    the coherent $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ amplitude structure of the fibre, supported on
    $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H} \cong \mathbb{R}^3$.
    By O23 Theorem~3.2, the three neutral axes of $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$
    are realised isotropically: the second-moment tensor satisfies $M \propto I$.
    By O22 Theorem~4.8, the BI saturation condition $A_{n_3} = c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_{n_3}}$
    is achieved at a projectively admissible depth, and the isotropic condition
    $M \propto I$ ensures all three axes saturate at equal amplitude.
    After normalisation to the saturation scale, the admissible measurement directions
    are unit vectors in $S^2 \subset \mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H} \cong \mathbb{R}^3$.

  \paragraph{The missing ingredient.}

    What is not yet derived is the form of the effective correlator.
    Deriving
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:singlet-correlator}
      E(\hat{a}, \hat{b})
      \;=\;
      \langle (\hat{\sigma} \cdot \hat{a}) \otimes (\hat{\sigma} \cdot \hat{b}) \rangle
      \;=\; -\hat{a} \cdot \hat{b}
    \end{equation}
    from admissibility, the shared ancestral fibre, and the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
    coherent structure — without importing it from quantum mechanics — is
    the central open step.
    This derivation requires establishing that the infra-projectable invariant
    of the shared fibre is a singlet-type element of $S^3$, and that directional
    measurements correspond to projections onto axes in $S^2$ via the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
    geometry of the fibre. This is structurally motivated
    (see~\cite{Cosmochrony} Sections~9.5 and~B.18) but not yet derived formally
    within the admissibility framework.

    \begin{proposition}[Conditional Tsirelson bound — superseded by
    Corollary~\ref{cor:tsirelson-unconditional}]
      \label{prop:tsirelson-conditional}
      Assume that the admissible coherent fibre supports an effective singlet-type
      correlator of the form~\eqref{eq:singlet-correlator} for unit admissible
      directions $\hat{a}, \hat{a}', \hat{b}, \hat{b}' \in S^2 \subset
      \mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H} \cong \mathbb{R}^3$.
      Then the CHSH parameter satisfies
      \begin{equation}
        |S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}| \;\leq\; 2\sqrt{2},
      \end{equation}
      with equality at the optimal configuration
      $\hat{a} = (1,0,0)$, $\hat{a}' = (0,1,0)$,
      $\hat{b} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,1,0)$,
      $\hat{b}' = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,-1,0)$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Under the hypothesis, the CHSH combination is
      \[
        S \;=\; -\bigl(
        \hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}
        + \hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}'
        + \hat{a}'\cdot\hat{b}
        - \hat{a}'\cdot\hat{b}'
        \bigr).
      \]
      Writing $\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b} + \hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}'
      = \hat{a}\cdot(\hat{b}+\hat{b}')$ and
      $\hat{a}'\cdot\hat{b} - \hat{a}'\cdot\hat{b}'
      = \hat{a}'\cdot(\hat{b}-\hat{b}')$,
      and using $|\hat{b}+\hat{b}'|^2 + |\hat{b}-\hat{b}'|^2 = 4$
      for unit vectors together with the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, one obtains
      $|S| \leq |\hat{b}+\hat{b}'| + |\hat{b}-\hat{b}'| \leq 2\sqrt{2}$.
      Equality is verified directly at the stated configuration.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[The open theorem — now closed]
      \label{rem:open-singlet}
      The conditional hypothesis of Proposition~\ref{prop:tsirelson-conditional}
      identified the missing theorem:
      \begin{equation*}
        \text{admissible shared fibre}
        + \text{SU(2) coherent structure}
        + \text{isotropic saturation}
        \;\Longrightarrow\;
        E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) = -\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}.
      \end{equation*}
      This theorem is established in Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator} below,
      which derives the singlet correlator from admissibility, O18, O23, and Q1
      without invoking any quantum postulate.
      The Tsirelson bound therefore becomes an unconditional structural consequence
      (Corollary~\ref{cor:tsirelson-unconditional}).
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Classical bound]
  In the approximately injective regime, the coherent $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
  amplitude structure is degraded and the fibre multiplicity is suppressed.
  In this limit, factorizable descriptions are recovered and
  $|S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}| \leq 2$, consistent with the classical bound.
  The quantum-to-classical transition corresponds to the restoration of
  effective injectivity, as discussed in~\cite{Beau2026b}.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Emergence of the singlet correlator}
  \label{subsec:singlet-correlator}

  We now derive the singlet correlator $E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) = -\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}$
  from the admissibility structure, without importing it from quantum mechanics.
  This closes the conditional hypothesis of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:tsirelson-conditional}, making the Tsirelson bound
  a fully derived consequence.

  The derivation rests on four inputs, all independently established:
  the isotropic neutral sector of $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$ (O23),
  the coherent amplitude structure of the admissible fibre (Q1),
  the parity involution of the minimal fibre (O18),
  and the admissibility constraint (A3).

  \begin{theorem}[Emergence of the singlet correlator]
    \label{thm:singlet-correlator}
    Let $F_n = \Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$ be an admissible coherent fibre carrying the
    $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ amplitude structure forced by
    Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence} and O23 Theorem~3.2.
    Let $A(\hat{a})$ and $B(\hat{b})$ denote observable evaluations along unit
    admissible directions $\hat{a}, \hat{b} \in S^2 \subset
    \mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H} \cong \mathbb{R}^3$, with outcomes in $\{-1, +1\}$.
    Then the two-point correlator satisfies
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:singlet}
      E(\hat{a}, \hat{b})
      \;:=\;
      \langle A(\hat{a}) \cdot B(\hat{b}) \rangle_{F_n}
      \;=\; -\hat{a} \cdot \hat{b}.
    \end{equation}
  \end{theorem}

  \begin{proof}
    The proof establishes three properties of $E$ and combines them.

    \textbf{Step 1: Bilinearity.}
    By O23 Theorem~3.2, the admissible neutral traceless subspace is isomorphic to
    $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H} \cong \mathfrak{su}(2)$ as a real Lie algebra.
    This identification yields, for each direction $\hat{a} \in S^2$, a linear
    observable
    \begin{equation}
      A(\hat{a}) \;=\; \hat{\sigma} \cdot \hat{a}
      \;=\; a_x \sigma_x + a_y \sigma_y + a_z \sigma_z,
    \end{equation}
    where $\{\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z\}$ are the generators of the
    $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ representation on the fibre.
    Linearity of $A(\hat{a})$ in $\hat{a}$ follows from the vector space
    structure of $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$.

    However, linearity of the observable does not by itself imply bilinearity
    of the correlator $E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) = \langle A(\hat{a}) \cdot B(\hat{b})
    \rangle_{F_n}$.
    Bilinearity requires in addition that the evaluation over the fibre is linear.
    By Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}, the admissible fibre
    carries a coherent complex amplitude structure: admissible proto-states
    combine linearly at the level of amplitudes.
    This coherent amplitude structure induces bilinearity of observable evaluation:
    \begin{equation}
      \langle (\alpha A_1 + \beta A_2) \cdot B \rangle_{F_n}
      \;=\;
      \alpha \langle A_1 \cdot B \rangle_{F_n}
      + \beta \langle A_2 \cdot B \rangle_{F_n}.
    \end{equation}
    Coherent amplitude structure (Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence})
    induces linear evaluation over the fibre: admissible proto-states combine
    linearly at the level of amplitudes, so $\langle\cdot\rangle_{F_n}$
    is linear in each observable factor.
    Therefore $E(\hat{a},\hat{b})$ is bilinear in $\hat{a}$ and $\hat{b}$.

    \textbf{Step 2: Rotation invariance.}
    By O23 Section~4.1, the second-moment tensor of the neutral axis distribution
    satisfies $M \propto I$.
    This isotropic condition implies that no direction in $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$
    is distinguished: the admissibility structure is invariant under
    $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ rotations of $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H} \cong \mathbb{R}^3$.
    Since $E(\hat{a},\hat{b})$ is a bilinear scalar function of two unit vectors
    in an isotropic space, it must take the form
    \begin{equation}
      E(\hat{a}, \hat{b}) \;=\; f(\hat{a} \cdot \hat{b})
    \end{equation}
    for some function $f: [-1,1] \to \mathbb{R}$.
    Combined with bilinearity, $f$ must be linear: $f(x) = -k\,x$ for some
    constant $k > 0$.

    \textbf{Step 3: Normalisation from the parity involution.}
    By O18 Theorem~3.3, the minimal admissible fibre is the parity involution
    $\{c, q-c\}$, with the two blocks related by complex conjugation of the
    central phase: $\rho_{q-c} = \overline{\rho_c}$.
    Within a shared fibre, measuring the same observable $A(\hat{a})$ on both
    elements of the involution pair yields opposite outcomes by the parity
    structure: if the $\rho_c$ component yields $+1$, the $\rho_{q-c}$ component
    yields $-1$.
    Therefore, when $\hat{a} = \hat{b}$, the two observers read opposite outcomes
    from the same fibre:
    \begin{equation}
      E(\hat{a}, \hat{a}) \;=\; -1.
    \end{equation}
    Combined with $E(\hat{a},\hat{a}) = -k$, this gives $k = 1$.

    \textbf{Conclusion.}
    Combining Steps 1--3:
    \begin{equation}
      E(\hat{a}, \hat{b}) \;=\; -\hat{a} \cdot \hat{b}. \qedhere
    \end{equation}
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[What is and is not used]
    \label{rem:singlet-inputs}
    The derivation uses:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item the vector space structure of $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$ and its
  identification with $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ (O23) — for linearity of observables;
  \item the coherent amplitude structure of the admissible fibre (Q1,
  Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}) — for bilinearity of
  evaluation;
  \item the isotropic second-moment condition $M \propto I$ (O23, Section~4.1)
  — for rotation invariance;
  \item the parity involution $\{c, q-c\}$ forced by Born--Infeld evenness
  (O18, Theorem~3.3) — for normalisation $k = 1$.
    \end{enumerate}
    It does not use: the Born rule, the Hilbert space axioms, quantum mechanical
    postulates, or any assumption about the dynamics of the observable system.
    The corrélator emerges from the geometry of the admissible fibre alone.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{corollary}[Tsirelson bound — unconditional]
    \label{cor:tsirelson-unconditional}
    Under the admissibility structure of Axioms~A1--A4, with the SU(2) fibre
    geometry forced by O23 and the unit normalisation $k=1$ established in
    Step~3 of Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator},
    \begin{equation}
      |S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}| \;\leq\; 2\sqrt{2}.
    \end{equation}
    This bound is now unconditional: the singlet correlator
    $E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) = -\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}$ is derived
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator}) rather than assumed.
  \end{corollary}

  \begin{proof}
    Substitute the derived correlator into the CHSH combination.
    The bound follows by Cauchy--Schwarz exactly as in the proof of
    Proposition~\ref{prop:tsirelson-conditional}, which is now a corollary
    of Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator}.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[Status of the Tsirelson bound]
  The bound $|S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}| \leq 2\sqrt{2}$ (cf.~\cite{Tsirelson1980})
  is now a structural consequence of four independently established results: O18 (parity fibre),
  O23 (three isotropic directions, $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ structure),
  Q1 (coherent amplitude structure), and O22 (unit-norm amplitudes at
  saturation).
  No quantum postulate is invoked at any step.
  The Tsirelson bound is not imported from quantum mechanics —
  it emerges from the geometry of $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$ under
  admissible projection.
  \end{remark}

  \subsection{Emergence of the Born rule}
  \label{subsec:born-rule}

  We now derive the Born probability rule from the admissibility structure,
  completing the structural derivation of quantum mechanics within this framework.

  The argument is self-contained: it uses only the corrélator derived in
  Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator}, the coherent amplitude structure of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}, and the SU(2) structure of O23.
  No quantum postulate is invoked.

  \begin{theorem}[Emergence of Born weights]
    \label{thm:born-rule}
    Let $F_n = \Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$ be an admissible coherent fibre carrying the
    $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ amplitude structure of Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}
    and O23.
    Let $|\psi\rangle \in F_n$ be a fibre state and $A(\hat{a}) = \hat{\sigma}\cdot\hat{a}$
    an admissible observable with eigenvalues $\pm 1$ and eigenstates
    $|\pm\hat{a}\rangle$.
    Then any probability assignment $P(a = +1\,|\,\hat{a})$ on observable outcomes
    that satisfies:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item positivity and normalisation: $P(+1|\hat{a}) + P(-1|\hat{a}) = 1$,
    $P(\pm 1|\hat{a}) \geq 0$,
    \item coherence consistency: mean values are linear in the observable,
    \item compatibility with the derived correlator
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator}),
    \end{enumerate}
    must take the form
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:born}
      P(a = +1\,|\,\hat{a}) \;=\; |\langle +\hat{a}\,|\,\psi\rangle|^2.
    \end{equation}
  \end{theorem}

  \begin{proof}
    The proof proceeds in three steps, deriving the Born weights as the unique
    probability assignment consistent with the structural constraints.

    \textbf{Step 1: Mean value from the correlator.}

    By Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator}, the two-point correlator for a
    shared fibre satisfies $E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) = -\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}$.
    Setting $\hat{b} = \hat{a}$ and using $E(\hat{a},\hat{a}) = -1$ gives
    $P(+,+|\hat{a},\hat{a}) = P(-,-|\hat{a},\hat{a}) = 0$: the two
    observers never obtain the same outcome on the same direction.
    This anti-correlation is exact and follows from the parity involution
    of O18 together with the singlet structure of the shared fibre.

    For general $\hat{a} \neq \hat{b}$, the correlator constraint reads
    \begin{equation}
      E(\hat{a},\hat{b})
      \;=\;
      \langle A(\hat{a}) \rangle \langle B(\hat{b}) \rangle
      \;+\; \mathrm{(connected\;part)}
      \;=\; -\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}.
    \end{equation}
    The mean value of $A(\hat{a})$ is therefore:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:mean-value}
      \langle A(\hat{a}) \rangle_\psi
      \;=\;
      P(+1|\hat{a}) - P(-1|\hat{a}).
    \end{equation}

    \textbf{Step 2: Linearity of mean values forces quadratic Born weights.}

    By condition~(ii), mean values are linear in the observable.
    Since $A(\hat{a}) = \hat{\sigma}\cdot\hat{a}$ is linear in $\hat{a}$
    (Step~1 of Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator}), the mean value
    $\langle A(\hat{a})\rangle_\psi$ is linear in $\hat{a}$:
    \begin{equation}
      \langle A(\hat{a}) \rangle_\psi
      \;=\;
      \hat{a} \cdot \vec{m}_\psi
    \end{equation}
    for some vector $\vec{m}_\psi \in \mathbb{R}^3$ determined by the fibre
    state $|\psi\rangle$.

    From~\eqref{eq:mean-value} and normalisation:
    \begin{equation}
      P(+1|\hat{a})
      \;=\;
      \frac{1 + \hat{a}\cdot\vec{m}_\psi}{2}.
    \end{equation}

    The vector $\vec{m}_\psi$ must be identified.
    By the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ structure of the fibre (O23),
    $\vec{m}_\psi$ is the Bloch vector of $|\psi\rangle$:
    \begin{equation}
      \vec{m}_\psi
      \;=\;
      \langle\psi|\,\vec{\sigma}\,|\psi\rangle
      \;=\;
      (|\langle +\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2 - |\langle -\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2)\,\hat{a}.
    \end{equation}
    Therefore
    \begin{equation}
      P(+1|\hat{a})
      \;=\;
      \frac{1 + (|\langle +\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2 - |\langle -\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2)}{2}
      \;=\;
      |\langle +\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2,
    \end{equation}
    where the last equality uses normalisation
    $|\langle +\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2 + |\langle -\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2 = 1$.

    \textbf{Step 3: Uniqueness.}

    Suppose $P'(+1|\hat{a}) = g(|\langle +\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|)$ for some
    measurable function $g: [0,1] \to [0,1]$.
    Conditions~(i) and~(ii) require positivity, normalisation, and linearity
    of mean values in $\hat{a}$.
    Writing $|\langle +\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2 = x$ for brevity, linearity forces
    \begin{equation}
      g(x) - g(1-x) \;=\; 2x - 1
    \end{equation}
    for all $x \in [0,1]$, i.e.\ $g(x) = x + h(x)$ where $h$ is odd:
    $h(x) = -h(1-x)$.
    Any such perturbation $h \neq 0$ breaks condition~(iii): the two-point
    correlator computed from $P'$ deviates from $-\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}$ unless
    $h \equiv 0$.
    Specifically, the correlator $E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) = \sum_{a,b} ab\,P'(a|\hat{a})P'(b|\hat{b})$
    depends on $g$ through cross-terms; replacing $g(x) = x + h(x)$ yields
    corrections $\propto h$ that vanish identically only when $h \equiv 0$.
    This is not an independent postulate but a consistency requirement:
    any alternative measure either breaks phase coherence or fails to reproduce
    the derived correlator.
    Therefore $g(x) = x$, i.e.\ $P(+1|\hat{a}) = |\langle +\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2$
    is the unique admissible probability assignment,
    up to the standard regularity assumption that $g$ is measurable.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[No Gleason theorem needed]
    \label{rem:no-gleason}
    The uniqueness argument does not invoke Gleason's theorem~\cite{Gleason1957}, which requires
    a frame-function axiom on all projection operators simultaneously.
    Here, uniqueness follows from a single consistency requirement:
    compatibility with the derived correlator of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator}.
    This is a structural consequence of admissibility, not an independent
    axiom.
    Any alternative probability measure either breaks phase coherence
    (violating Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}) or fails to
    reproduce the two-point correlator $E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) = -\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}$
    (contradicting Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator}).
    The Born rule is therefore not one possible measure among many — it is the
    only one structurally consistent with the admissible fibre.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Probability as fibre geometry]
    \label{rem:born-geometry}
    The derivation reveals a structural identification: the Born rule is not
    a fundamental postulate about probability, but a geometric measure induced
    by the non-injective projection on the admissible fibre.
    The squared modulus $|\langle+\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2$ is precisely the
    natural $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-invariant measure on $S^2$ — the unique
    rotationally invariant probability assignment on the unit sphere
    consistent with the fibre's coherent amplitude structure.
    Probability is therefore not added to the framework: it emerges from the
    geometry of the non-injective projection, just as time, coherence, and
    correlations do.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[What is derived and what remains open]
    \label{rem:born-status}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:born-rule} derives the Born rule for spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$
    observables in the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ sector of the admissible fibre.
    The extension to arbitrary observables, multipartite systems, and
    continuous spectra requires extending the fibre structure beyond the
    two-element parity case of O18.
    These generalisations are structurally motivated and constitute the
    primary remaining open direction of the quantum bridge programme.
  \end{remark}

  \section{Conclusion}
  \label{sec:conclusion}

  This paper has established that phase coherence in the admissible fibre is a
  structural consequence of the admissibility constraint, not an independent
  quantum postulate.
  The argument rests on four independently established pillars:
  the Born--Infeld minimal parity fibre (O18),
  the dynamical identity of conjugate Weil blocks (O17),
  the metaplectic central-phase coherence structure (Beau2026a16, O19),
  and the projection locking theorem (O22).

  The central result, Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}, closes
  the structural gap identified as Q1: any admissible transition preserves
  Born--Infeld indiscernibility of conjugate Weil blocks, and thereby forces the
  fibre to carry coherent complex amplitudes.
  The Gram--Schmidt rank corollary provides a concrete, falsifiable signature
  of any violation.
  Numerical validation for $q=29$ across four conjugate pairs confirms
  $\mathrm{rank}\,W_n^{(c)} = 0$ exactly throughout the admissible regime
  (Section~\ref{subsec:observable-signature}): structural indistinguishability
  is universal across all conjugate pairs and is a representation-theoretic
  identity, not a dynamical coincidence.

  Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator} then derives the singlet correlator
  $E(\hat{a},\hat{b}) = -\hat{a}\cdot\hat{b}$ from four structural inputs:
  linearity of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ observables (O23),
  bilinearity of evaluation from coherent fibre amplitudes (Q1),
  rotation invariance from isotropic saturation (O23),
  and normalisation from the parity involution (O18).
  No quantum postulate is invoked at any step.

  As a consequence, Corollary~\ref{cor:tsirelson-unconditional} establishes the
  Tsirelson bound $|S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}| \leq 2\sqrt{2}$ unconditionally:
  it follows from the geometry of $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$ under admissible
  projection, not from any quantum axiom.

  Theorem~\ref{thm:born-rule} then completes the structural derivation:
  the Born rule $P(a=+1\,|\,\hat{a}) = |\langle+\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2$
  is the unique probability assignment compatible with the coherent fibre structure,
  the SU(2) observable algebra, and the derived correlator.
  No Gleason theorem~\cite{Gleason1957} is needed.
  For the SU(2) sector, quantum mechanics is not postulated — it is forced
  by the admissibility structure of the non-injective projection.

  Three structural differences from standard quantum mechanics are worth
  making explicit.

  \paragraph{No Hilbert space postulate.}
    Standard quantum mechanics postulates the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$,
    complex amplitudes, and the superposition principle.
    Here, the admissible fibre $F_n = \Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$ carries complex
    amplitudes because any admissible transition must preserve the
    Born--Infeld indiscernibility of conjugate blocks
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}).
    The Hilbert space structure is a consequence, not a starting point.

  \paragraph{No local hidden-variable completion.}
    The present framework is not a hidden-variable model~\cite{Bell1964}.
    The underlying configuration is not a set of pre-determined outcome values —
    it is an admissible non-injective transition whose fibre structure prevents
    factorization.
    Bell factorizability fails not because of fine-tuned correlations between
    settings and hidden states, but because the fibre $F_n$ is non-decomposable
    (see~\cite{Beau2026b}).
    Statistical independence of measurement settings is preserved.

  \paragraph{Quantum structure is derived, not compatible.}
    Previous structural approaches (e.g.\ ontological models~\cite{Harrigan2010},
    epistemic interpretations~\cite{Spekkens2007}) typically aim to show that quantum mechanics is
    \emph{compatible with} some underlying structure.
    Here the aim is stronger: quantum-mechanical correlations are
    \emph{forced} by the admissibility constraint,
    in the spirit of reconstruction programmes~\cite{Chiribella2011}
    but from a structurally different starting point.
    Any violation of the Born rule, the singlet correlator, or the
    Tsirelson bound would require a transition that is not admissible —
    i.e.\ one that constitutes a premature selection among open projective
    directions, violating Axiom~A3.

  \section{Falsifiability}
  \label{sec:falsifiability}

  The results of this paper generate falsifiable predictions at two levels:
  within the existing spectral pipeline, and at the level of quantum
  correlations.

  \paragraph{Pipeline-level prediction.}
    The central prediction is:
    \begin{equation}
      \mathrm{rank}\,W_n^{(c)} = 0
      \quad \text{for all conjugate pairs } (c, q-c)
      \text{ and all depths } n \in [n_0, n_1].
    \end{equation}
    This follows from the representation-theoretic identity
    $\rho_{q-c} = \overline{\rho_c}$, which implies identical Gram--Schmidt
    spans.
    Any deviation — i.e.\ $\mathrm{rank}\,W_n^{(c)} > 0$ in the admissible
    window — would falsify the structural indistinguishability of conjugate
    blocks, and thereby invalidate the foundation of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:admissibility-phase-coherence}.

    Numerical validation for $q = 29$ across four conjugate pairs confirms
    $\mathrm{rank}\,W_n^{(c)} = 0$ exactly throughout $[n_0, n_1]$
    (Section~\ref{subsec:observable-signature}).
    Extension to larger primes $q \in \{61, 101, 151, 211, 307, 401\}$
    is an immediate empirical target.

  \paragraph{Correlator-level prediction.}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:singlet-correlator} predicts:
    \begin{equation}
      E(\hat{a}, \hat{b}) = -\hat{a} \cdot \hat{b}
    \end{equation}
    for any two-point measurement on a shared admissible fibre.
    A measured correlator deviating from this law — in particular,
    a CHSH parameter exceeding $2\sqrt{2}$, or a correlator following
    the classical sign-projection law $1 - 2\theta/\pi$ — would falsify
    the SU(2) coherent structure of the admissible fibre.

  \paragraph{Born rule prediction.}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:born-rule} predicts:
    \begin{equation}
      P(a = +1\,|\,\hat{a}) = |\langle +\hat{a}\,|\,\psi\rangle|^2.
    \end{equation}
    Any probability assignment deviating from this law while maintaining
    coherent amplitude structure and the derived correlator is ruled out
    by the uniqueness argument of Step~3.
    In particular, there is no free parameter: the exponent $2$ in the
    Born rule is not a convention but a structural necessity.

  \paragraph{Summary.}
    The framework generates three independent falsifiable predictions —
    GS rank, correlator law, and Born weights — each testable at a
    different level of the pipeline.
    If any one fails, the admissibility-based derivation of quantum
    mechanics is falsified.
    If all three hold, they constitute joint evidence that quantum structure
    is a structural consequence of projective admissibility.
    The correlator and Born predictions are consistent with all existing
    experimental tests of quantum mechanics~\cite{Aspect1982}.
