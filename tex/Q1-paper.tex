  \begin{abstract}
    We prove an exact, unconditional identity in the finite Weil (oscillator) representation of the
    Heisenberg group $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, $q$ an odd prime: for every complete
    (inversion-symmetric) Breadth-First-Search shell of the Cayley graph
    $\mathrm{Cay}(\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}), S_q)$, the orbit vectors generated by
    conjugate Weil sectors~$c$ and~$q-c$ span identical subspaces of $\mathbb{C}^q$, for both the
    special character block and generic character triples. The proof reduces every orbit vector to a
    single pure Fourier mode and shows the reachable frequency set is closed under negation whenever
    the generating shell is inversion-symmetric --- an unconditional, coherence-independent fact,
    verified independently by $1{,}548$ exact integer-arithmetic checks across six primes with zero
    failures, and by a negative control confirming the criterion correctly detects failure on a
    deliberately truncated shell. We further characterise precisely which diagnostics this rigidity
    forecloses: not only Fourier support, subspace rank, projectors, dimensions, and principal angles,
    but any quantity invariant under independent rephasing and reindexing of the underlying frame ---
    a class that includes normalised Bargmann-type invariants, proved trivial here by a general
    frame-orbit argument, not only by numerical instance. A bibliography search across the finite
    Weil/oscillator representation literature confirms the underlying conjugation identity
    $\rho_{q-c}=\overline{\rho_c}$ is standard; no matching result was found in the literature
    surveyed for the exact subspace-equality identity or its no-go corollaries. This paper corrects
    an earlier version (v1.1.2) that used this construction to claim a chain of results from
    projective admissibility to the singlet correlator, the Tsirelson bound, and the Born rule;
    independent review found the central theorem's published proof unsound, and separately found
    defects in the Born-rule proof independent of that chain. This revision withdraws that
    programme: the singlet correlator and Tsirelson bound survive only as a standard, explicitly
    conditional textbook remark, and the Born rule is withdrawn outright. What remains is the exact
    rigidity result above and its precisely scoped no-go corollaries.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
  \label{sec:introduction}

  This paper proves a rigidity theorem in finite representation theory and draws its precise,
  narrow consequence for a class of diagnostics one might otherwise have hoped to use to distinguish
  conjugate sectors of the finite Weil representation. It does not derive quantum-mechanical
  structure, and makes no claim about physical admissibility or projection dynamics.

  \subsection*{Origin and status of this paper}

    This paper originated as an attempt, within the Cosmochrony non-injective-projection
    framework~\cite{Cosmochrony} and its spectral admissibility sub-programme (the O-series), to
    derive quantum-mechanical phase coherence, the singlet correlator, the Tsirelson bound, and the
    Born rule from a projective admissibility constraint (v1.1.2, Zenodo
    \texttt{10.5281/zenodo.19561060}). Independent review of that version found its central
    theorem's published proof unsound: the coherence-regime-dependent lemma it relied on is
    contradicted once a Breadth-First-Search truncation bug in the numerical pipeline is fixed, and
    the theorem's admissibility argument separately relied on an external fibre-structure result
    (O18) subsequently found invalid. Independent review of the Born-rule derivation found further,
    independent defects in its own proof. Full detail is recorded in
    Remark~\ref{rem:v2-changes} and Section~\ref{subsec:born-rule}.

    What survives, and is the actual content of this paper, is narrower and unconditional rather than
    coherence-dependent: a representation-theoretic rigidity theorem about the object the numerical
    pipeline actually computes (Definition~\ref{def:three-objects}), together with a precisely
    characterised no-go corollary. This paper does not claim to close any open problem of the
    Cosmochrony programme; it documents, precisely, what one specific construction in that programme
    can and cannot show.

  \subsection*{Main result}

    \textbf{Exact Fourier-support rigidity} (Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity}). For every complete
    BFS shell of $\mathrm{Cay}(\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}), S_q)$, conjugate Weil-sector
    fingerprint vectors span identical subspaces of $\mathbb{C}^q$ --- both for the special character
    block $(c,0,0)$ and for generic character triples $(c_1,c_2,c_3)$. This is proved unconditionally,
    independent of any coherence measure, by an exact five-step argument
    (Section~\ref{subsec:exact-theorem}), and verified independently by $1{,}548$ integer-arithmetic
    checks across six primes with zero failures (Section~\ref{subsec:numerical-verification}).

    \textbf{No-go corollaries} (Section~\ref{subsec:no-go}). No diagnostic built from Fourier
    support, subspace rank, projectors, dimensions, or principal angles can separate the sectors
    (Corollary~\ref{cor:support-rank-nogo}). More precisely, and correcting an earlier, over-stated
    formulation of this corollary: no diagnostic invariant under independent rephasing and
    reindexing of the underlying frame can either (Corollary~\ref{cor:frame-nogo}) --- a class that
    includes normalised Bargmann-type invariants but explicitly excludes raw, phase-gauge-dependent
    quantities such as an unnormalised Gram matrix entry.

  \subsection*{What this paper does not claim}

    This paper does not derive Hilbert-space structure, superposition, measurement outcomes, the
    singlet correlator, the Tsirelson bound, the Born rule, or non-locality (contrast
    Section~\ref{sec:comparison-qm}). It does not claim that the abstract object $V_n^{(c)}$ of
    v1.1.2's original definition, or the delta-vector probe of O17, coincide with the pipeline object
    $\widehat V_n^{\mathbf c}$ this paper's theorem is actually about --- that identification is left
    open (Remark~\ref{rem:open-bridge}). It does not claim that a single Weil/Heisenberg source
    produces multiple, genuinely phase-distinguishable quantum-like frames; a direct check found no
    such structure recoverable within this construction (see the Conclusion's open directions). It
    does not claim priority beyond the literature actually surveyed (see the Conclusion's Related
    Work).

  \subsection*{Organisation}

    Section~\ref{sec:admissibility-phase-coherence} states the setup and the three distinct objects
    that must not be conflated (Section~\ref{subsec:q1-setup}), the exact theorem and its proof

  \section{Exact Fourier-Support Rigidity of Conjugate Weil Sectors}
  \label{sec:admissibility-phase-coherence}

  This section establishes an exact, unconditional identity: the Fourier support of a specific
  numerical pipeline's fingerprint vectors is identical between conjugate Weil sectors~$c$ and~$q-c$,
  for every complete Breadth-First-Search (BFS) shell of the Heisenberg Cayley graph, both for the
  special character block used by the original pipeline and for generic character triples
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity}). A corollary characterises exactly which diagnostics ---
  support, rank, subspace projectors, dimensions, principal angles, and any rephasing/permutation-
  invariant Gram-derived quantity --- are structurally blind to this distinction
  (Corollaries~\ref{cor:support-rank-nogo} and~\ref{cor:frame-nogo}). Numerical verification
  independent of the analytic proof is reported in
  Section~\ref{subsec:numerical-verification}.

  \paragraph{What this section does not claim.}
    The identity proved here concerns a precisely specified pipeline object
    (Definition~\ref{def:three-objects}, object~(3)), not the abstractly-defined $V_n^{(c)}$ of the
    v1.1.2 theorem this revision withdraws, nor O17's own probe vector. The identification between
    these objects is an open question, not assumed anywhere below (Remark~\ref{rem:open-bridge}).
    This section makes no claim about physical admissibility, projection dynamics, or
    quantum-mechanical structure (see Remark~\ref{rem:not-admissibility-not-cancellation}).

  \subsection{Setup: three distinct objects}
    \label{subsec:q1-setup}

    We work on the Cayley graph $\mathrm{Cay}(G_q, S_q)$, $G_q = \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$
    for an odd prime~$q$, elements written $(a,b,\gamma)$ with group law
    $(a,b,\gamma)(a',b',\gamma') = (a+a',\, b+b',\, \gamma+\gamma'+ab') \bmod q$, and standard
    symmetric generating set $S_q = \{X, X^{-1}, Y, Y^{-1}\}$, $X=(1,0,0)$, $Y=(0,1,0)$. The Weil
    representation $\rho_c$, $c \in \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$, acts on functions of
    $x \in \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$ by
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:weil-matrix-element}
      \bigl[\rho_c(a,b,\gamma)\bigr](x)
      \;=\;
      \frac{1}{\sqrt q}\,
      e^{\frac{2\pi i}{q} c \left(\gamma + b(x-a)\right)}.
    \end{equation}

    \begin{definition}[Complete BFS shell]
      \label{def:complete-shell}
      A BFS shell $S_n \subset \mathrm{Cay}(G_q,S_q)$ at depth~$n$ is \emph{complete} if it is the
      full set of group elements at graph distance exactly~$n$ from the identity --- i.e.\ the BFS
      traversal producing it was not stopped by a node-count quota partway through generating that
      depth.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{lemma}[Shell inversion symmetry]
      \label{lem:shell-inversion}
      Every complete shell $S_n$ satisfies $S_n = S_n^{-1} := \{u^{-1} : u \in S_n\}$.
    \end{lemma}
    \begin{proof}
      $S_q$ is symmetric ($S_q = S_q^{-1}$), so graph distance from the identity is
      inversion-invariant: a length-$n$ path $e = g_0, g_1, \dots, g_n = u$ using generators
      $t_1,\dots,t_n \in S_q$ gives, by inverting and reversing, a length-$n$ path from~$e$ to
      $u^{-1}$ using generators $t_n^{-1}, \dots, t_1^{-1} \in S_q$. Hence $d(e,u) = d(e,u^{-1})$
      for every $u \in G_q$, so the complete sphere of radius~$n$ is closed under inversion.
    \end{proof}

    For $u \in S_n$ and generators $s_1,s_2,s_3 \in S_q$, form the intermediate elements
    $u_1 = u s_1$, $u_2 = u_1 s_2$, $u_3 = u_2 s_3$, with $b$-coordinates
    $b_1 = b_u + b_{s_1}$, $b_2 = b_1 + b_{s_2}$, $b_3 = b_2 + b_{s_3}$
    (additive, since the group law's $b$-coordinate carries no cross term). For a character triple
    $\mathbf{c} = (c_1,c_2,c_3)$, the \emph{$k=3$ fingerprint vector} at combinatorial index
    $(u; s_1,s_2,s_3)$ is
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:fingerprint}
      \psi^{\mathbf{c}}_{u;s_1,s_2,s_3}(x)
      \;=\;
      \bigl[\rho_{c_1}(u_1)\bigr](x)\,
      \bigl[\rho_{c_2}(u_2)\bigr](x)\,
      \bigl[\rho_{c_3}(u_3)\bigr](x),
    \end{equation}
    and we define
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:vhat-def}
      \widehat V_n^{\mathbf{c}}
      \;:=\;
      \mathrm{span}\bigl\{\, \psi^{\mathbf{c}}_{u;s_1,s_2,s_3} \;:\; u \in S_n,\ s_1,s_2,s_3 \in S_q \,\bigr\}
      \;\subset\; \mathbb{C}^q.
    \end{equation}
    The special block used by the original numerical pipeline is $\mathbf{c} = (c,0,0)$; we write
    $\widehat V_n^{(c)} := \widehat V_n^{(c,0,0)}$.

    \begin{definition}[Three distinct objects]
      \label{def:three-objects}
      Three objects, notationally and logically distinct, must not be conflated below.
      \begin{enumerate}[(1)]
        \item \textbf{Q1's original abstract object} (v1.1.2, notation unchanged):
          $V_n^{(c)} := \mathrm{span}\{\rho_c(g)\, v_0 : g \in B_n\}$ for an \emph{unspecified} probe
          $v_0$ and cumulative ball $B_n$.
        \item \textbf{O17's own probe}~\cite{Beau2026a21}:
          $v_0(c,b_1,b_2) := \rho_c(b_1,b_2,0)\, e_0 = \omega^{c\, b_2 b_1}\, e_{b_1}$, an explicit
          \emph{delta} (single-nonzero-entry) vector.
        \item \textbf{The pipeline's actual object}, $\widehat V_n^{\mathbf{c}}$, defined in
          \eqref{eq:vhat-def}: every entry of every fingerprint vector has identical magnitude
          $q^{-3/2}$ --- the signature of applying $\rho_{\mathbf c}$ to the \emph{uniform} state, not
          to a delta basis vector.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{definition}

    \begin{remark}[The identification is open]
      \label{rem:open-bridge}
      Whether $\widehat V_n^{\mathbf{c}}$ (object~(3)) coincides with $V_n^{(c)}$ (object~(1)) under
      some choice of $v_0$, or with the span generated from O17's own probe (object~(2)), is not
      established and is not assumed anywhere below. The uniform-probe structure of
      $\widehat V_n^{\mathbf{c}}$ is what makes Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity} true (Remark
      \ref{rem:uniform-probe-essential}); a delta-vector construction would not obviously reduce to
      pure Fourier modes, and the argument below would not directly apply to it.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{definition}[Metaplectic central-phase coherence]
      \label{def:coherence-length}
      For a character $c$ and BFS shell $S_n$, the central-phase coherence length is
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:coherence-length}
        \ell_\gamma^{(c)}(n)
        \;=\;
        \left|
          \frac{1}{|S_n|}
          \sum_{g \in S_n} e^{2\pi i c \gamma_g / q}
        \right|.
      \end{equation}
      This quantity is retained as a definition only; unlike in v1.1.2, no result below is
      conditioned on its value, since Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity} holds unconditionally.
    \end{definition}

  \subsection{Exact theorem}
    \label{subsec:exact-theorem}

    \begin{theorem}[Exact conjugate-sector Fourier-support identity]
      \label{thm:exact-rigidity}
      Let $q$ be an odd prime and let $S_n$ be a complete BFS shell of $\mathrm{Cay}(G_q, S_q)$
      (Definition~\ref{def:complete-shell}). For every character triple
      $\mathbf{c}=(c_1,c_2,c_3) \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^3$,
      \begin{equation}
        \widehat V_n^{\mathbf{c}} \;=\; \widehat V_n^{-\mathbf{c}},
      \end{equation}
      where $-\mathbf{c} = (-c_1,-c_2,-c_3) \bmod q$. In particular
      $\widehat V_n^{(c)} = \widehat V_n^{(q-c)}$ for the special block $\mathbf{c}=(c,0,0)$.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      \textbf{Step 1 (pure Fourier modes).}
      Expanding~\eqref{eq:weil-matrix-element} in~\eqref{eq:fingerprint}, the $x$-dependent factor of
      $\psi^{\mathbf{c}}_{u;s_1,s_2,s_3}$ is $e^{2\pi i K x / q}$ with
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:frequency}
        K(u;s_1,s_2,s_3;\mathbf{c})
        \;=\;
        (c_1{+}c_2{+}c_3)\, b_u
        + (c_1{+}c_2{+}c_3)\, b_{s_1}
        + (c_2{+}c_3)\, b_{s_2}
        + c_3\, b_{s_3}
        \pmod q,
      \end{equation}
      so $\psi^{\mathbf{c}}_{u;s_1,s_2,s_3} = \lambda \cdot e_K$ for a unimodular scalar~$\lambda$
      (carrying the $\gamma$-coordinate dependence) and the standard Fourier basis vector
      $e_K(x) = q^{-1/2} e^{2\pi i K x/q}$. Distinct-frequency modes are orthonormal, so
      \begin{equation}
        \widehat V_n^{\mathbf{c}} \;=\; \mathrm{span}\{ e_K : K \in F_{\mathbf{c}} \},
        \qquad
        F_{\mathbf{c}} := \{ K(u;s_1,s_2,s_3;\mathbf{c}) : u \in S_n,\ s_1,s_2,s_3 \in S_q \},
      \end{equation}
      an exact combinatorial (integer) set determining $\widehat V_n^{\mathbf{c}}$, not a
      floating-point approximation.

      \textbf{Step 2 (conjugation negates the frequency).}
      By~\eqref{eq:frequency}, $K$ is linear and homogeneous in~$\mathbf{c}$, so
      $K(u;s_1,s_2,s_3;-\mathbf{c}) = -K(u;s_1,s_2,s_3;\mathbf{c})$, hence
      $F_{-\mathbf{c}} = -F_{\mathbf{c}} := \{-K \bmod q : K \in F_{\mathbf{c}}\}$.

      \textbf{Step 3 (shells are inversion-symmetric).}
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:shell-inversion}, $u \in S_n \implies u^{-1} \in S_n$, and group inversion
      $(a,b,\gamma)^{-1}=(-a,-b,ab-\gamma)$ negates the $b$-coordinate.

      \textbf{Step 4 (the generating set is $b$-antisymmetric).}
      The generators' $b$-coordinates are $\{b_X, b_{X^{-1}}, b_Y, b_{Y^{-1}}\} = \{0,0,1,-1\}$,
      itself closed under negation.

      \textbf{Step 5 (matching index).}
      Combining Steps~3--4: for every $(u;s_1,s_2,s_3)$ there is a matching index
      $(u^{-1}; s_1', s_2', s_3')$ in the same index set with $b_{u^{-1}} = -b_u$ and each
      $b_{s_i'} = -b_{s_i}$. Since $K$ is linear in $(b_u,b_{s_1},b_{s_2},b_{s_3})$ (Step~1), negating
      all four inputs negates~$K$:
      \begin{equation}
        K(u^{-1};s_1',s_2',s_3';\mathbf{c}) \;=\; -K(u;s_1,s_2,s_3;\mathbf{c}) \pmod q.
      \end{equation}
      Hence $F_{\mathbf{c}}$ is itself closed under negation: $F_{\mathbf{c}} = -F_{\mathbf{c}} =
      F_{-\mathbf{c}}$ (Step~2), so $\widehat V_n^{\mathbf{c}} = \widehat V_n^{-\mathbf{c}}$ exactly.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[The uniform probe is essential]
      \label{rem:uniform-probe-essential}
      Step~1's reduction to a pure Fourier mode uses that every fingerprint vector arises from
      applying $\rho_{\mathbf c}$ to the same fixed input (the implicit uniform state), so the
      $x$-dependence is carried entirely by the matrix element~\eqref{eq:weil-matrix-element}. A
      delta-vector probe (object~(2) of Definition~\ref{def:three-objects}) would instead produce
      position-localised, not frequency-localised, orbit vectors, and Steps~1--5 would not apply to it
      without a separate argument.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{No-go corollaries: which diagnostics are blind to the conjugate pair}
    \label{subsec:no-go}

    \begin{corollary}[Support/rank/subspace no-go]
      \label{cor:support-rank-nogo}
      Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity}, no diagnostic built from the Fourier
      support, the rank of $\widehat V_n^{\mathbf{c}}$, the rank of any residual subspace
      $\widehat V_n^{\mathbf{c}} \cap (\widehat V_n^{-\mathbf{c}})^\perp$, subspace projectors,
      subspace dimensions, or principal angles between $\widehat V_n^{\mathbf{c}}$ and
      $\widehat V_n^{-\mathbf{c}}$ can distinguish the two sectors: all such quantities are functions
      of the pair of subspaces alone, and Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity} makes that pair identical.
    \end{corollary}

    A sharper question is whether any diagnostic built from the raw \emph{frame vectors} themselves
    --- not merely the subspace they span --- can separate the sectors. A raw Gram-matrix entry
    depends on an arbitrary choice of phase convention for each frame vector, so the right notion of
    ``diagnostic'' is one invariant under that choice.

    \begin{lemma}[Frame-orbit rigidity]
      \label{lem:frame-orbit-rigidity}
      Write the raw fingerprint vectors as $x_i = a_i\, e_{K_i}$ (Step~1 of
      Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity}'s proof), so the Gram matrix is
      $G_{ij} = \langle x_i, x_j\rangle = \overline{a_i}\, a_j\, \delta_{K_i,K_j}$. Consider the
      action $G \mapsto D^* P^* G P D$ for $D \in U(1)^N$ (independent rephasing of each frame vector)
      and $P \in S_N$ (reindexing). The orbit of~$G$ under this action is completely determined by
      the unordered multiset $\{(\mathrm{block}_i, |a_i|)\}_{i=1}^N$, where $\mathrm{block}_i$ is the
      equivalence class of~$i$ under $K_i = K_j$ --- because $G$ exposes only $\delta_{K_i,K_j}$
      (never the value of~$K_i$) and $|a_i|$ (never its phase).
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      $D,P$ act on $G$ exactly as stated; $|D_{ii}|=1$ removes each $a_i$'s phase from any
      $D$-invariant quantity while preserving $|a_i|$, and $P$ permutes the index set while
      preserving which indices coincide in~$K$. Conversely, any two Gram matrices of this
      block-diagonal form with the same $(\mathrm{block}_i,|a_i|)$ multiset are related by such a
      $(D,P)$, since within each frequency block the vectors are pairwise parallel and can be rephased
      into a common real, positive-definite rank-one block, and blocks may be freely relabelled by~$P$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[Frame no-go]
      \label{cor:frame-nogo}
      The frames underlying $\widehat V_n^{\mathbf{c}}$ and $\widehat V_n^{-\mathbf{c}}$ lie in the
      same $(D,P)$-orbit, hence carry the identical invariant of
      Lemma~\ref{lem:frame-orbit-rigidity}. Consequently, any diagnostic invariant under individual
      rephasing and reindexing of the frame --- the natural formalisation of ``a diagnostic that does
      not itself privilege a phase convention or vector ordering'' --- is blind to the $\mathbf{c}$ vs
      $-\mathbf{c}$ distinction. This does \emph{not} extend to quantities that depend on an
      arbitrarily fixed phase gauge (a raw, unnormalised Gram entry is exactly such a quantity, and is
      not claimed invariant).
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      Conjugation sends $x_i \mapsto \overline{x_i} = \overline{a_i}\, e_{-K_i}$ pointwise (immediate
      from~\eqref{eq:weil-matrix-element}: conjugating $\rho_{\mathbf c}$ is the same as evaluating
      $\rho_{-\mathbf{c}}$). Hence $|a_i|$ is unchanged and $K_i \mapsto -K_i$, a bijection of
      $\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$, so $K_i = K_j \iff -K_i = -K_j$: the partition into blocks is exactly
      preserved (blocks may be relabelled, which is invisible to the orbit invariant of
      Lemma~\ref{lem:frame-orbit-rigidity}). The two sectors' frames therefore share the identical
      $(\mathrm{block}_i,|a_i|)$ multiset, hence lie in the same $(D,P)$-orbit.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{example}[Normalised Bargmann triples]
      \label{ex:bargmann}
      For $i,j,k$ with $G_{ij}, G_{jk}, G_{ki} \neq 0$, the normalised Bargmann invariant
      $B(i,j,k) := G_{ij}G_{jk}G_{ki} / (|G_{ij}||G_{jk}||G_{ki}|)$ is rephasing-invariant by
      construction. For pure Fourier lines it evaluates to
      $B(i,j,k) = |a_ia_ja_k|^2\, \delta_{K_i,K_j}\delta_{K_j,K_k}\delta_{K_k,K_i} / |a_ia_ja_k|^2 = 1$
      on a colinear triple ($K_i=K_j=K_k$) and is undefined (zero denominator) whenever any pair is
      orthogonal --- verified numerically at $q=29$: a genuinely colinear triple gives $B=1$ exactly
      in both the $c=3$ and $q-c=26$ sectors; a triple containing an orthogonal pair gives $B=0$ in
      both, to machine precision.
    \end{example}

  \subsection{Numerical verification}
    \label{subsec:numerical-verification}

    Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity} and Corollaries~\ref{cor:support-rank-nogo}
    and~\ref{cor:frame-nogo} are proved analytically above; this section reports independent
    numerical verification, in three parts.

    \paragraph{(a) Multi-prime exact verification.}
      For every prime $q \in \{13,17,29,37,53,61\}$, every complete BFS shell, the special block
      $\mathbf{c}=(c,0,0)$ for $c \in \{1,3,7,11\}$ (as available) and several generic triples
      $(c_1,c_2,c_3)$, the exact frequency-set criterion $F_{\mathbf c} = F_{-\mathbf c}$ was checked
      by direct integer computation (\texttt{code/exact\_fourier\_support.py}). Result: $1{,}548$
      (shell, block) checks, $0$ failures.

    \paragraph{(b) Numeric cross-validation ($q=29$).}
      Independently, the numeric SVD-based residual rank of
      $\widehat V_n^{(c)} \cap (\widehat V_n^{(q-c)})^\perp$ was compared against the exact
      criterion at every depth, for the four conjugate pairs $\{(1,28),(3,26),(7,22),(11,18)\}$
      (\texttt{code/test\_regression.py}): exact agreement throughout, residual rank exactly zero at
      every complete shell.

      Figure~\ref{fig:separator} shows, for each pair, the central-phase coherence

    \paragraph{(c) Negative control: the truncation artefact.}
      The v1.1.2 pipeline's BFS routine stopped mid-shell once a node-count quota was reached,
      silently keeping a partial final shell. Such a shell need not satisfy
      Lemma~\ref{lem:shell-inversion} and so is not covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity}.
      Reconstructing that pre-correction behaviour (\texttt{code/weil\_pipeline\_compat.py}) and
      re-running the exact/numeric comparison confirms: the exact criterion and the numeric residual
      rank agree throughout, \emph{including} at the truncated shell, where both correctly register
      $F_{\mathbf c} \neq F_{-\mathbf c}$ and a non-zero residual (e.g.\ $q=29$, depth~$15$). This is
      the exact mechanism behind the single non-zero data point in the v1.1.2 figure: a BFS-quota
      truncation breaking inversion symmetry, not a floating-point saturation effect as the v1.1.2
      text claimed, and not a boundary of validity of the theorem, which concerns complete shells
      only by hypothesis.

  \subsection{Scope of this result}
  \label{subsec:q1-consequences}

  \begin{remark}[A support-rigidity result, not a physical rule]
    \label{rem:not-admissibility-not-cancellation}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity} and its corollaries establish an exact structural rigidity of
    Fourier support under character conjugation --- a fact about which subspaces of $\mathbb{C}^q$
    certain vector families span, and about which diagnostics of those subspaces and frames can or
    cannot see. They are neither a physical admissibility rule governing which projective transitions
    are realised, nor a statement about destructive cancellation between admissible contributions to
    a shared observable event. Coherent non-detection of a configuration, if any such notion applies
    elsewhere in the programme, may result from either mechanism; the two should not be conflated.
    This paper establishes only the representation-theoretic rigidity above.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Changes from v1.1.2]
    \label{rem:v2-changes}
    This is v2.0.0, a major revision. Independent review of v1.1.2 (Zenodo
    \texttt{10.5281/zenodo.19561060}) found its central theorem's published proof unsound: the
    coherence-regime-dependent lemma underlying it is contradicted by a corrected BFS pipeline (part
    (c) above), and the theorem's own admissibility argument relied on an external fibre-structure
    result subsequently found invalid. This revision withdraws that theorem, the associated
    Gram--Schmidt rank corollary (dimensionally impossible as stated: both summands already live in
    $\mathbb{C}^q$, so no rank overflow beyond~$q$ can occur), and the singlet-correlator, Tsirelson,
    and Born-rule derivations that depended on it or on since-compromised external results
    (Section~\ref{subsec:tsirelson} below). In their place, this revision proves an unconditional
    structural identity (Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity}) about a precisely specified object
    (Definition~\ref{def:three-objects}), narrower in scope but exact rather than
    coherence-conditional, together with a precisely characterised no-go corollary
    (Corollaries~\ref{cor:support-rank-nogo}, \ref{cor:frame-nogo}). This paper no longer claims to
    derive quantum-mechanical structure; see Section~\ref{sec:comparison-qm}.
  \end{remark}

  \subsection{Singlet correlator and Tsirelson bound: a standard conditional remark}
  \label{subsec:tsirelson}

  Neither the singlet correlator nor the Tsirelson bound is a result of this paper. Both survive
  v1.1.2 only as a well-known conditional derivation, stated here for completeness and explicitly
  demoted from ``theorem'' to ``remark'': its hypotheses already encode most of the quantum structure
  being sought, and are established by neither this paper (bilinearity previously cited the withdrawn
  Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity}'s v1.1.2 predecessor for linearity of fibre evaluation) nor O23
  (isotropy is imported from another paper, not derived there --- see the discussion accompanying
  Definition~\ref{def:three-objects} and the withdrawal notice, Remark~\ref{rem:v2-changes}).

  \begin{remark}[Standard conditional derivation]
    \label{rem:singlet-tsirelson-standard}
    Suppose (H1) admissible outcomes are evaluated by a fibre pairing $\langle\cdot\rangle_{F}$
    bilinear in two observables $A(\hat a) = \hat\sigma\cdot\hat a$, $B(\hat b)=\hat\sigma\cdot\hat b$
    built from the real Lie algebra identification $\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H} \cong \mathfrak{su}(2)$
    (standard mathematics, independent of O23 --- see e.g.~\cite{Hurwitz1898} and any standard
    reference on quaternions and $\mathrm{SU}(2)$); (H2) the pairing is isotropic, i.e.\ invariant
    under simultaneous $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ rotation of $\hat a,\hat b$; (H3)
    $E(\hat a,\hat a) := \langle A(\hat a) B(\hat a)\rangle_F = -1$. None of (H1)--(H3) is derived by
    this paper or by O23; they are stated here as named hypotheses, not established inputs. Under
    them, bilinearity plus isotropy force $E(\hat a,\hat b) = f(\hat a\cdot\hat b)$ for linear~$f$
    (Schur's lemma / the classification of bilinear invariants of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$),
    and (H3) fixes $f(x)=-x$, giving the textbook singlet correlator
    $E(\hat a,\hat b) = -\hat a \cdot \hat b$. The Tsirelson bound
    $|S_{\mathrm{CHSH}}| \leq 2\sqrt 2$ then follows immediately by Cauchy--Schwarz, exactly as in the
    standard derivation~\cite{Tsirelson1980}: writing
    $S = -(\hat a\cdot(\hat b+\hat b') + \hat a'\cdot(\hat b -\hat b'))$ and using
    $|\hat b+\hat b'|^2+|\hat b-\hat b'|^2=4$ gives $|S|\le 2\sqrt2$, with equality at the standard
    CHSH-optimal configuration. This is textbook algebra once (H1)--(H3) are granted; nothing about
    it is a contribution of the present paper.
  \end{remark}

  \subsection{Born rule: open}
  \label{subsec:born-rule}

  \begin{remark}[Born rule not derived]
    \label{rem:born-status}
    v1.1.2 claimed a derivation of the Born rule (Theorem ``Emergence of Born weights''). Independent
    review found the proof unsound independently of its dependence on the withdrawn main theorem: it
    identified the mean-value vector $\vec m_\psi$ with the quantum expectation
    $\langle\psi|\vec\sigma|\psi\rangle$, importing the very rule being derived; it then equated the
    full Bloch vector $\vec m_\psi$ with its own projection along the single, arbitrarily chosen
    measurement axis $\hat a$; and its uniqueness step computed the two-point correlator from a
    \emph{factorised} joint probability $P'(a|\hat a)P'(b|\hat b)$, which cannot reproduce a
    non-factorisable (entangled) correlator's uniqueness without already assuming the classical
    limit it is meant to exclude. This revision withdraws that argument entirely rather than
    retaining a weakened, conditional version of it. The Born rule is not derived by this paper and
    remains, as far as this programme is concerned, fully open.
  \end{remark}

  \section{Conclusion}
  \label{sec:conclusion}

  This paper proves an exact, unconditional identity: for complete BFS shells of the Heisenberg
  Cayley graph, conjugate Weil-sector fingerprint vectors span identical subspaces of
  $\mathbb{C}^q$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity}), for both the special character block and
  generic character triples. The proof is representation-theoretic and combinatorial: it reduces
  every fingerprint vector to a pure Fourier mode and shows the reachable frequency set is closed
  under negation whenever the generating shell is inversion-symmetric. Two corollaries characterise
  exactly which diagnostics this rigidity puts out of reach: support, rank, subspace projectors,
  dimensions, and principal angles (Corollary~\ref{cor:support-rank-nogo}); and, more precisely,
  any diagnostic invariant under individual rephasing and reindexing of the underlying frame
  (Corollary~\ref{cor:frame-nogo}), a class that includes normalised Bargmann-type invariants but
  explicitly excludes raw, phase-gauge-dependent quantities. Both the analytic proof and independent
  numerical verification (Section~\ref{subsec:numerical-verification}: $1{,}548$ exact checks across
  six primes, zero failures; a negative control confirming the criterion correctly detects failure on
  a deliberately truncated shell) support the result.

  This paper does not derive quantum-mechanical structure. v1.1.2 claimed a chain from admissibility
  to phase coherence to the singlet correlator, the Tsirelson bound, and the Born rule; independent
  review found the central theorem's proof unsound and, separately, defects in the Born-rule proof
  independent of that chain (Remark~\ref{rem:v2-changes}, Section~\ref{subsec:born-rule}). This
  revision withdraws that programme. The singlet correlator and Tsirelson bound survive only as a
  standard, explicitly conditional textbook remark (Section~\ref{subsec:tsirelson}); the Born rule is
  withdrawn outright and remains fully open.

  \subsection*{Related work}

    A bibliography search across the finite Weil/oscillator representation literature (Gurevich--
    Hadani and collaborators; Cruickshank, Guti\'errez Frez and Szechtman's Weil-model constructions;
    the harmonic/group-frame literature; and Terras and collaborators' spectral study of Heisenberg
    Cayley graphs~\cite{Terras2002heis}, whose eigenvalue plots exhibit an unremarked $s \leftrightarrow
    q-s$ mirror symmetry at the level of scalar spectra, never at the level of eigenvectors or spanned
    subspaces) confirms $\rho_{-c} = \overline{\rho_c}$ is standard; no matching result was found in
    the literature surveyed for either the exact subspace-equality identity of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity} or its no-go corollaries. This is a statement about the coverage
    of the search conducted, not a claim that no such result exists.

  \subsection*{Open directions}

    \paragraph{The three-object bridge.}
      Whether the pipeline's uniform-probe object $\widehat V_n^{\mathbf c}$
      (Definition~\ref{def:three-objects}, object~(3)) coincides with Q1's original abstract
      $V_n^{(c)}$ (object~(1)) under some choice of probe, or with the span generated by O17's own
      delta probe (object~(2)), remains open (Remark~\ref{rem:open-bridge}).

    \paragraph{Precise scope of the frame no-go.}
      Corollary~\ref{cor:frame-nogo} is proved for the exact pure-Fourier-line frame structure of
      this pipeline. Whether an analogous rigidity holds for other Weil-representation orbit
      constructions not reducible to a single frequency per vector is not addressed here.

    \paragraph{Genuine multi-basis emergence.}
      Whether a single Weil/Heisenberg source can produce multiple, genuinely phase-distinguishable
      quantum-like frames is not resolved by this paper, and is not recoverable within this
      construction: a direct check (cross-Gram and normalised Bargmann invariants between conjugate
      sectors) found no richer phase geometry than the rigidity already proved, for the same
      structural reason (each vector reduces to a single-phase Fourier line). A future attempt would
      need non-conjugate character blocks, BFS-depth holonomy, or a frame construction not reducible
      to a single Fourier mode.

  \section{Comparison with standard quantum mechanics}
  \label{sec:comparison-qm}

  Unlike v1.1.2, this paper does not derive Hilbert-space structure, superposition, measurement
  outcomes, the correlator law, the Tsirelson bound, the Born rule, or non-locality; those claims are
  withdrawn (Remark~\ref{rem:v2-changes}). The comparison below is correspondingly modest.

  \paragraph{A cautionary result for the programme's own diagnostic toolkit.}
    Support, rank, and Gram-derived quantities are natural, representation-theoretic diagnostics, and
    one might plausibly have expected some such diagnostic to separate conjugate Weil sectors --- it
    is what v1.1.2 attempted to construct.
    Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity} and its corollaries show this expectation fails for a structural
    reason, not a numerical coincidence: the pure-Fourier-line reduction that proves the identity
    simultaneously forecloses the richer phase geometry that would be needed to separate the sectors
    by any rephasing/permutation-invariant frame diagnostic. This is worth recording as a cautionary
    example for any future use of support/rank/Gram-based diagnostics elsewhere in the spectral
    admissibility programme.

  \paragraph{The singlet correlator and Tsirelson bound are standard, not derived here.}
    Section~\ref{subsec:tsirelson} records the textbook derivation of $E(\hat a,\hat b)=-\hat a\cdot
    \hat b$ and the Tsirelson bound from bilinearity, isotropy, and normalisation, explicitly as named,
    unproved hypotheses rather than as a result of this framework.

  This section makes no further use of Table references or of the withdrawn theorems; see
  Remark~\ref{rem:v2-changes} for the full list of what v1.1.2 claimed and why each claim is
  withdrawn in this revision.

  \section{Exact scope and reproducible checks}
  \label{sec:falsifiability}

  This paper's central claim is mathematical, not a physical prediction; ``falsifiability'' in the
  usual physics sense does not apply. This section instead separates the exact proof from its
  independent numerical verification and negative control, so each can be checked on its own terms.

  \paragraph{Exact proof and its hypotheses.}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-rigidity} holds under three explicit hypotheses: $q$ is an odd prime; the
    BFS shell $S_n$ is complete in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:complete-shell} (inversion-
    symmetric, Lemma~\ref{lem:shell-inversion}); and the vectors in question are the pipeline's
    uniform-probe fingerprint construction, object~(3) of Definition~\ref{def:three-objects} ---
    not Q1's abstract $V_n^{(c)}$ or O17's delta probe. No coherence, saturation, or admissibility
    hypothesis is used anywhere in the proof.

  \paragraph{Numerical verification.}
    Independent of the proof: $1{,}548$ exact (integer-arithmetic) checks across
    $q \in \{13,17,29,37,53,61\}$, both the special block and generic character triples, zero
    failures; and a numeric SVD cross-check against the exact criterion at $q=29$, exact agreement
    throughout (Section~\ref{subsec:numerical-verification}, parts (a)--(b)).

  \paragraph{Negative control.}
    The exact criterion is not vacuously true: reconstructing the pre-correction, quota-truncated
    BFS (which breaks Lemma~\ref{lem:shell-inversion}'s hypothesis) produces shells on which both the
    exact criterion and the independent numeric computation correctly and simultaneously register
    failure (Section~\ref{subsec:numerical-verification}, part~(c)). This is also the exact mechanism
    behind v1.1.2's one reported non-zero residual, previously misattributed to floating-point
    saturation.

  \paragraph{Open numerical extension.}
    Extension to larger primes ($q \in \{101, 151, 211, 307, 401\}$, following the O25 prime range)
    would extend the empirical base but is not required by the proof, which holds for every odd
    prime unconditionally.
